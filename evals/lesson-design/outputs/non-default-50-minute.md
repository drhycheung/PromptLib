**Assumptions made** (complete mode — no clarifications sought):

- Cohort: undergraduate students with basic essay-writing experience but no formal training in peer feedback.
- Platform: synchronous online session (e.g. Zoom/Teams) with breakout rooms and a shared document tool (e.g. Google Docs or Padlet).
- Language: English as a medium of instruction; students are L2 English users.
- No scheduled break (50 minutes does not warrant one).
- Focus: giving and receiving written peer feedback on short paragraphs, not oral presentation feedback.

# Lesson Plan: Practising Peer Feedback in English

## 1. Lesson Context

| Field | Details |
|---|---|
| **Topic** | Giving and receiving written peer feedback in English |
| **Discipline and cohort** | General English or academic writing; undergraduate students (approximately Year 1–2) |
| **Prior knowledge** | Students can write a short paragraph; no prior structured peer feedback training assumed |
| **Duration** | 50 minutes |
| **Breaks** | None scheduled (50-minute session) |
| **Constraints and resources** | Synchronous online; breakout rooms required; shared document tool (Google Docs or equivalent); short draft paragraphs prepared in advance by students |

---

## 2. Lesson Intended Learning Outcomes

| ID | Measurable outcome | Single Bloom level | Observable evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| LILO 1 | **Identify** the features of effective and ineffective peer feedback comments in sample texts | Understand | Correctly categorise ≥ 3 of 4 sample comments during the whole-class activity |
| LILO 2 | **Apply** a structured feedback framework (content, organisation, language) to evaluate a peer's paragraph | Apply | Written feedback comment submitted in the shared document, covering all three framework dimensions |
| LILO 3 | **Evaluate** the usefulness of feedback they receive and select one specific revision to act on | Evaluate | Written reflection note stating which feedback point they accept, why, and what revision they will make |

---

## 3. Formative Assessment

| Assessment activity | Learner evidence | Formative use or feedback | Aligned LILO(s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Comment sorting task** — students classify four sample feedback comments as effective or ineffective and explain why (whole-class poll + brief discussion) | Poll responses and verbal/chat justifications | Teacher highlights patterns immediately; class co-constructs a short checklist of effective feedback features | LILO 1 |
| **Peer feedback writing** — in breakout pairs, each student writes structured feedback on their partner's paragraph using the COL framework | Written comments in a shared Google Doc (one document per pair) | Partner reads the comment; teacher circulates across rooms and leaves written marginal prompts where feedback is vague or praise-only | LILO 2 |
| **Reflection note** — individually, each student writes 2–3 sentences stating which piece of feedback they find most useful and what revision they will make | Typed note in their own section of the shared document | Teacher reads notes asynchronously and provides a brief whole-class debrief in the final 5 minutes; students leave the lesson with a concrete next action | LILO 3 |

## 4. Teaching and Learning Sequence

| Time | Segment | Teacher activity | Learner activity | Resources | Aligned assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–5 min | **Orientation: Why peer feedback?** | Share screen with two short student paragraphs and contrasting feedback comments. Ask: *"Which comment would help you improve — and why?"* | Students read silently; share initial reactions in chat | Slides with sample paragraphs and comments | — |
| 5–12 min | **Mini-input: The COL framework** | Present a 5-slide overview of the Content–Organisation–Language (COL) framework with one annotated model comment per dimension. Keep to 7 minutes maximum. | Students copy the three-dimension checklist into their own notes | Slides; printable/downloadable COL checklist (shared via chat link) | — |
| 12–20 min | **Comment sorting task** (whole class) | Display four sample comments (2 effective, 2 ineffective). Run a poll (Mentimeter or Zoom poll). After responses, cold-call two or three students to justify. Synthesise into a class checklist on screen. | Vote in the poll; explain reasoning aloud or in chat; suggest additions to the checklist | Poll tool; shared whiteboard or slide with checklist draft | Comment sorting task → LILO 1 |
| 20–37 min | **Breakout peer feedback writing** | Assign pairs, share the Google Doc template. Circulate across rooms (2 min each). Leave a marginal prompt comment if a student's feedback is vague, e.g. *"Can you add a specific example from the paragraph?"* | Read partner's paragraph carefully; write structured feedback addressing all three COL dimensions; aim for 5–8 sentences | Google Doc template (one per pair, pre-populated with student names and paragraph slots); COL checklist | Peer feedback writing → LILO 2 |
| 37–44 min | **Individual reflection note** | Return to main room. Prompt: *"Read your partner's comments. Choose the most useful one. In 2–3 sentences: What is the feedback? Do you agree? What specific change will you make?"* | Write reflection note in the designated section of the shared document | Same Google Doc; prompt on screen | Reflection note → LILO 3 |
| 44–50 min | **Closure and debrief** | Display 2–3 anonymised reflection notes (with permission or pre-selected). Ask: *"What made their peer useful to them?"* Summarise key lesson takeaways. Preview next steps (e.g. revision draft due). | Share observations; receive whole-class feedback; note revision plan | Screen-shared anonymised notes | Links LILO 1 + 3 |

> **Total: 50 minutes.**

## 6. Assumptions and Follow-up Choices

1. **Student drafts prepared in advance.** The plan assumes students have submitted a short paragraph (100–150 words) before the lesson. If not, the orientation and sorting task can be compressed and 5 minutes added at the start for in-lesson drafting — though this reduces time for the core feedback activity.
2. **Pair assignment.** Random breakout pairing is assumed. If students have mixed English proficiency, consider intentional pairing (similar level) or cross-level pairing depending on your pedagogical goal.
3. **COL framework.** If your course uses a different framework (e.g. PQRS, TAG), replace the mini-input slides and checklist accordingly — the timing remains the same.
4. **Asynchronous follow-up.** The reflection note doubles as a written record; teacher review is post-lesson. If you want in-lesson feedback on LILO 3, the closure debrief can incorporate a quick show-of-hands or emoji reaction to each anonymised note.
5. **Platform.** Google Docs is assumed; substitute with Microsoft Word (shared via Teams) or a Padlet if preferred — adjust sharing instructions accordingly.
