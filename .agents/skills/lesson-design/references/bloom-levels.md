# Bloom Level Guide

Use this guide when choosing the cognitive level for each lesson intended learning outcome. Select the level from the evidence the learner must produce during the lesson, not from the topic.

## Levels and sentence stems

| Level | What the learner does | Sentence stems | Typical evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remember | Retrieves and recalls information | Define, list, name, recall, state, recognise, label | Correct answers in a low-stakes quiz, labelled diagram, accurate summary |
| Understand | Explains ideas in own words | Explain, summarise, interpret, describe, illustrate, classify | Annotated example, oral explanation, concept map, categorised cases |
| Apply | Uses a procedure or principle in a familiar situation | Apply, use, solve, execute, demonstrate, administer, construct | Completed worksheet, produced artefact, worked solution, structured feedback |
| Analyse | Breaks information into parts and examines relationships | Analyse, compare, differentiate, examine, organise, attribute, deconstruct | Comparison table, annotated data set, diagnosis of an error, classified scenarios |
| Evaluate | Judges the quality of information or a strategy against criteria | Evaluate, critique, justify, defend, assess, referee, judge | Written judgement with reasons, review of a draft, recommendation with trade-offs |
| Create | Produces a new or original artefact or plan | Design, construct, develop, plan, compose, formulate, produce | Original lesson plan, prototype, model, portfolio, new procedure |

## Choosing one level

1. Identify the verb that describes what the learner demonstrably does at the end of the lesson.
2. Check that the formative assessment requires that verb. If the assessment only asks learners to recognise or list, the outcome is not at a higher level.
3. Keep the level realistic for the time available. A 50-minute lesson rarely supports sustained work at Create unless the task is tightly scoped.
4. Split an outcome that contains several verbs into separate LILOs, or keep the highest level actually demanded by the task and assessment.

## Common errors

| Problem | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Combined label | “Remember/Understand” | Choose one level, usually the higher one the task demands |
| Level inflation | “Evaluate critically and create an innovative design” | Split into two LILOs, or lower the level to match the assessment |
| Unobservable verb | “Understand the nature of assessment” | “Classify six assessment examples by purpose and timing” |
| Mismatch with assessment | Outcome says Evaluate but the task asks learners to list features | Align the task with the outcome, or lower the outcome to Apply |
| Coverage too low | Every LILO is Understand | Vary levels across outcomes and match them to the cognitive demand of the lesson |
